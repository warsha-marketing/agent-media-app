# Scripts use Targeted Diacritics and Delivery Tags, not full تشكيل

The spec first required a fully diacritized Script, to stop the voice guessing vowels. In the first live Product Hero test (2026-09-23, RUMI Royal Rituals, Levantine, ElevenLabs `eleven_v3`), full تشكيل made the delivery slow and formal — closer to فصحى than spoken Levantine — while a fully unmarked Script mis-read exactly the words that carry the sale: جلد as *jald* (whipping) instead of *jild* (leather), مسك as *masak* (took) instead of *misk*. Both were nouns in a bare list of fragrance notes, where no sentence disambiguates them. So a Script is written in plain dialect spelling with Targeted Diacritics — a mark on every word a voice could misread (product nouns, notes, ingredients and words with a common second reading), and nowhere else — plus a few Delivery Tags (`[softly]`, `[excited]`, …) that `eleven_v3` treats as direction rather than speech. The user can add a mark to any word they hear mis-read and re-voice.

## Consequences

- The "fully diacritized" check on generated Scripts is replaced by a check that the Script is Arabic text plus allowed Delivery Tags, and that every word a voice could misread is marked. No validator can know every such word, so this is enforced two ways: the writer reports the product terms it used and marked (checked against the Script, whatever their hamza or alef spelling), and a growing list of known misreadable words (homographs) is refused unmarked whatever the writer reported. Native reviewers extend that list when they hear a mis-read; it lives in `HOMOGRAPHS` in `services/api-v2/src/drafts/script-check.ts`.
- A user's edited Script is held only to the Delivery Tag rules (no unknown tags, no stray brackets) and must have Arabic to speak; it may carry Latin words such as a brand name, and the user judges their own marks.
- Captions and any Script display to viewers must strip Delivery Tags; the TTS alignment includes the tag characters.
- Delivery Tags depend on a TTS model that honours them (`eleven_v3`); a Voice on another model would speak them aloud.
